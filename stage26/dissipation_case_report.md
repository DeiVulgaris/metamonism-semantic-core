# Stage 26 — Semantic Case Study: Dissipation

## Question

What is dissipation, and how do its meanings differ across A, B, and C?

## A — Ontological-core-of-AGI

Dissipation is a mandatory architectural consequence after structural closure is broken. The registered source also connects it with counteracting global refixation.

Formalization: `post_unfold(S) -> dissipation_required(S)`

## B — UFCPS

Diss is an operator in the procedural flow `diff -> fix -> diss -> unfold`. Its registered effect is removal of exclusive dependence on the current carrier. The source distinguishes diss from destruction/deletion.

## C — ONTODYNAMICS

Dissipation is a processual/model-layer construct involving redistribution into new degrees of freedom. Orthogonality is treated as a minimal channel. Orthogonal Dissipation is canonical only within ONTODYNAMICS.

Its physical/cosmological applications have mixed epistemic status, including draft hypotheses and model-specific mappings.

## Cross-inventory result

**PARTIAL.**

A structural family resemblance is visible, but the corpus does not establish identity or mathematical isomorphism among A/B/C dissipation.

It also does not establish that C physical hypotheses prove A ontological claims or that B procedural diss proves C physical models.

This case demonstrates that the semantic core can preserve both:
1. a cross-source comparison;
2. the unresolved boundary preventing semantic collapse.
